Learning without a map

Hasan Alseba · Updated 13 November 2026 · 2 min read

I started studying physics and mathematics on my own in seventh grade. I did not have a clear plan, but I kept learning.

Finding a direction

When I learned about Olympiads, I wanted to try them. My preparation was haphazard. I studied general physics and pure mathematics, but I was often unsure what to study next.

In 2024, when I was in ninth grade, I entered Mawhoob astronomy and astrophysics. I earned a bronze medal in the first stage, then second place and a silver medal at the first camp. During my preparation, I also began helping teammates with spherical astronomy.

Stepping away, then returning

I later left the astronomy team because of personal circumstances. During that gap, I turned toward the physics Olympiad and entered the National Science and Math Olympiad (NSMO). In 2026, after passing all four stages, I returned to the national astronomy team.

Studying and teaching spherical astronomy

I studied Fundamental Astronomy to improve my understanding of the subject. I also wrote a spherical astronomy handout for my teammates, covering the geometry and coordinate systems used in astronomy problems.

The handout and recorded lectures are available here for other students to use.

Teaching my teammates

I like explaining where the mathematics or physics in an astronomy problem comes from. Sometimes, after an explanation, a teammate says, “Aha, that’s where it comes from!” That is my favourite part of teaching.
