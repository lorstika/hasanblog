Learning without a map

Hasan Alseba · Updated 13 November 2026 · 2 min read

My route into astronomy began with independent study—and grew through explaining the mathematics to other students.

Finding a direction

I started learning physics and mathematics in seventh grade. Discovering Olympiads gave me something to work toward, but my preparation was still haphazard: I studied general physics and pure mathematics on my own, without a carefully arranged curriculum.

Mawhoob gave that independent study a setting in which to develop. In 2024, when I was in ninth grade, I earned a bronze medal in its first astronomy stage, followed by second place and a silver medal at the first camp. Spherical astronomy became a subject in which I could help my teammates.

Stepping away, then returning

I later left the astronomy team because of personal circumstances. During that gap, I turned toward the physics Olympiad and entered the National Science and Math Olympiad (NSMO). In 2026, after passing all four stages, I returned to the national astronomy team.

Putting the mathematics into words

I continued studying Fundamental Astronomy and supporting other students. I wrote a spherical astronomy handout that moves from great circles and rotations to astronomical coordinates, collecting material I had been helping my peers understand.

That work is available in the spherical astronomy handout and recorded lectures. It connects my independent study with the work I can share with others.

“Aha, that’s where it comes from!”

I enjoy explaining the mathematics and physics behind an astronomy problem, or the astronomical concept that makes it work. My favourite moment is when a teammate responds, “Aha, that’s where it comes from!” Hearing that connection click is what I like most about teaching.
