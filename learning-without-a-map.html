Journey / THE NOTEBOOK

Learning without a map

Hasan Alseba · Based on my learning journey · 2 min read

My first path into physics and mathematics was messy and self-directed. I kept following it.

Starting in seventh grade

I began learning physics and mathematics in seventh grade. After discovering Olympiads, I started studying general physics and pure mathematics on my own. There was no carefully arranged curriculum: the learning was often haphazard, but I continued.

Over time, I built a broader foundation in physics and mathematics. That independent study supported the astronomy I would explore later.

A route into astronomy

I entered the Mawhoob astronomy and astrophysics national Olympiad pathway. I earned a bronze medal in the first stage, then placed second with a silver medal at the first camp.

Alongside my own preparation, I helped fellow students with spherical astronomy. I later stepped away from the team because of personal circumstances.

Returning through NSMO

During that gap, I aimed toward the physics Olympiad and entered the National Science and Math Olympiad. I passed all four stages and returned to the national astronomy team.

I continued helping my peers, wrote a spherical astronomy handout, and studied Fundamental Astronomy to strengthen my understanding. The handout and related teaching material are now part of the work I share here.

Where I hope to go next

I hope to study physics at a leading university, with particular interest in Caltech, ETH Zurich, and UCL.

My current goals are to apply to the KAUST Saudi Research Science Institute (SRSI), participate in the KAUST Math Competition (KMC), and work toward representing Saudi Arabia at the International Olympiad on Astronomy and Astrophysics (IOAA).

These are goals I am working toward; this page does not claim admission to SRSI, a KMC result, or completed IOAA participation.
